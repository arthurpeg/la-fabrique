Abstract 10 
 11 
Unlike previous studies examining the association between crude oil and renewable energy stock 12 
prices under average conditions, we employ a quantile -based regression approach offering a 13 
more comprehensive dependence structure under diverse market condition s. Using weekly data 14 
covering crude oil prices (WTI market) and three clean energy stock indices (the Wilderhill 15 
Energy Index, MAC Global Solar Energy Index, and S&P Global Clean Energy Index), quantile 16 
regression analyses provide solid evidence of the dec reasing dependence of clean energy stock 17 
returns on crude oil returns. The lagged effect of WTI oil returns on clean energy stock returns is 18 
generally significant, which indicates that clean energy stock returns react differently to new 19 
information on oil returns under different market conditions. We further check for asymmetrical 20 
effects of oil returns on clean energy stock returns in various market conditions and find a strong 21 
effect of negative oil returns during bearish periods and an insignificant effe ct during bullish 22 
episodes.   23 
Keywords: crude oil; clean energy stock indices; quantile regression; lagged and asymmetric 24

1. Introduction 29 
 30 
Crude oil has been one of the most important fuels for energy creation over the past decades. 31 
However, due to concerns regarding deteriorating global climatic conditions, there is a drastic 32 
switch to renewable or clean energy sources (such as solar, wind etc.) [1 -2]. Based on the latest 33 
report from the International Energy Agency (IEA), there will be a substan tial fall in crude oil 34 
demand by the end of 2024 and, consequently, an upward shift in renewable energy usage.  35 
Luqman et al. [3] find evidence of asymmetric impacts of renewable energy on economic growth 36 
in Pakistan. Razmi et al. [4] examine the associatio n of renewable energy consumption with 37 
stock market value and reveal evidence that stock market value influences renewable energies in 38 
the long term. Rahman and Velayutham [5] explore the association between renewable and non -39 
renewable energy consumption a nd economic growth and show evidence of a one -way Granger 40 
causality flowing from economic growth to renewable energy consumption. 41 
Recently, companies functioning in clean energy sectors have received considerable attention 42 
from investors not only because t heir stocks offer higher returns than general stocks but also 43 
because investments in clean energy companies have positive environmental and socio-economic 44 
impacts that potentially help ensure a certain degree of sustainability.  Kumar et al. [6] argue that 45 
investments in renewable energy sectors increase due to concerns about climate change and oil 46 
price volatility. Reboredo [7] contends that uncertainty in oil market encourages investors to 47 
capitalize in renewable energy stocks.  48 
Given the importance of the  association between the crude oil and clean energy sectors, a major 49 
line of literature studies the correlations between these two assets. Sadorsky [8] finds that an 50 
upsurge in oil prices increases the risk associated with clean energy equities. Kumar et al. [6] 51 
document how stock prices of renewable energy firms are sensitive to oil price shocks. 52 
Employing a copula approach, Reboredo [7] shows that the dependence between oil and clean 53 
energy stock prices evolve over time. Bondia et al. [9] indicate a short-term linkage between oil 54 
and renewable energy equity markets and, more importantly, show that the Granger -causality 55 
runs from commodity to stock markets. Using wavelets, Reboredo et al. [10] demonstrate that, 56 
although the short -run connection be tween energy and clean energy stock prices appears to be 57 
weak, such a relationship seems strong in the long run. Ahmad [11] finds that oil prices and 58 
renewable energy stock returns move in tandem, implying that an upturn in energy prices leads 59 
to an increa se in the stock prices of alternative energy firms. More recently, Ferrer et al. [12] 60 
3 
 
examine spillover measures between US clean energy stock returns and crude oil returns, and 61 
show that spillovers are significant in the short term and vary with time. Koc aarslan and Soytas 62 
[13] apply a nonlinear auto -regressive distributed lag (NARDL) model and report evidence of a 63 
nonlinear relationship and asymmetric effects between crude oil prices and clean energy stock 64 
prices. Xia et al. [14] examine the extreme conne ctedness between energy price and renewable 65 
energy stock indices, indicating evidence of a substitution relationship between dirty energy 66 
resources and renewable energy. Bouri et al. [1] show that crude oil acts as safe -haven asset for 67 
clean energy stocks during episodes of extreme market movement1. However, most of the above-68 
mentioned studies overlook the possibility that the effect of crude oil prices on clean energy 69 
indices can vary across the various quantiles of the return distribution.  70 
In this paper we examine the relationship between crude oil prices and clean energy stock 71 
indices, considering three aspects. First, we adopt  a quantile regression model to examine how 72 
variations in crude oil prices impact clean energy stock returns under diverse market  conditions. 73 
Second, we explore the lagged effect of the oil market on the stock index at various quantiles. 74 
Third, we investigate the nonlinear association between oil prices and clean energy stock indices. 75 
We contribute in two major ways. Firstly, unlike  previous researchers who use the ordinary least 76 
squares (OLS) method to examine the relationship between crude oil and stock indexes under 77 
average conditions, we apply a quantile regression (QR), proposed by  Koenker and Bassett [15], 78 
to reveal a more comprehensive picture of the relationship under diverse market conditions.  79 
Given that investors‟ heterogeneity is important to clean energy stock pricing, a heterogeneity -80 
consistent approach such as the QR needs to be estimated 2. This is based on the ratio nale that 81 
dependent variables across the return distribution  might react differently to similar shocks given 82 
investors‟ heterogeneity in the market. Since the application of OLS regression, which focuses 83 
on variations in means only, would produce biased es timates, QR has recently been employed in 84 
several studies [9, 16-20]. Secondly, this is the first study to investigate whether the impacts of 85 
crude oil returns on clean energy stock indexes are persistent (i.e. have lags), which allows us to 86

The main findings provide solid evidence for the decreasing dependence of clean energy stocks 96

negative oil returns during bearish periods and an insignificant association throughout bullish 98

However, using the quantile regression, we reveal the structure of the investment pattern of clean 245

returns. However, during downturns market conditions, clean energy stock returns have high 249 
dependency on crude oil returns, suggesting that investors should develop an investment decision 250 
to invest in clean energy stocks in bearish market conditions. During the Global Financial Crisis 251

clean energy stock indices rose significantly. Our analy sis helps break down the price change 253

4.2. Lagged impact of crude oil returns on clean energy stock returns 257 
 258 
The gradual information diffusion hypothesis is accepted under two conditions: first, if the 259 
lagged effect of crude oil returns on clean energy stock returns has a similar or a larger 260 
magnitude than the contemporaneous e ffect of crude oil returns; and second, if the lagged effect 261 
peaks then declines as the lag length increases. Earlier studies [26-27, 32] have applied those two 262 
conditions for testing the gradual information diffusion hypothesis. We first consider the ECO 263 
index and the results of Equation (7) are shown in Table 3. The OLS results show that the effect 264 
of lag 1 is positive. The gradual information diffusion hypothesis is rejected given that the lagged 265 
effect (i.e. β1) is lower than the contemporaneous effect (i.e. β0). Furthermore, the effect of lag 5 266 
is higher than the effect of lag 1. This suggests that investors have a delayed reaction to 267 
information about oil returns.  268 
 269 
Furthermore, we notice that the estimated results for the QR are quite similar to the O LS results 270 
in extreme quantiles (τ =0.05, 0.1, 0.9, 0.95). The lagged effects are highly positive, specifically 271 
in bullish or bearish markets. Whereas, there is an inverse relationship between the first lag of 272 
WTI and ECO during normal market conditions (i n the middle quantile). Checking for the 273 
diffusion hypothesis, the first condition is rejected. The second condition does not comply with 274 
our quantile regression model, given evidence that in lower quantiles the model fits perfectly to 275 
the condition that t he first lag has the highest effect on ECO prices. At the median quantiles, 276 
however, the fifth and sixth lags have the highest effect, indicating that during normal market 277 
conditions investors take five to six weeks to react to changes in oil prices. There fore, as the 278 
market conditions shift from bearish to bullish, the information diffusion shifts from t -1 to t -6 279 
indicating that investors in ECO react late to WTI prices. Figure 5 shows the quantile lagged 280 
impacts of oil returns on ECO returns. These results are comparable to those reported in previous 281 
studies [26-27, 32]. 282

4.4. Implications of the results 439 
 440 
We have revealed that oil price shocks have a strong positive impact on clean energy stock 441 
indices at lower quantiles, which complements previous findings [1, 9, 12 -14, 31]. Furthermore, 442 
the impact tends to decrease  at upper quantiles, which suggests a str ong association between oil 443 
prices and clean energy stock indices during bearish market periods. Economically speaking, the 444 
implication of this result matters to the investors operating in clean energy markets who should 445 
be highly concerned about variation s in the global oil price index when the market is bearish. 446 
Given that bearish market conditions refer to periods of low or negative returns, shifting towards 447 
clean energy investment in response to oil price movements appears to be common for hedging 448 
purposes.   449 
To further explain the reason behind such high dependency of clean energy stock movements on 450 
oil prices during bearish periods, we examine the asymmetric relationship to check the nonlinear 451 
association between clean energy stock indices and crude oi l prices and show evidence of high 452 
asymmetries in the lower quantiles and insignificant asymmetries in the upper quantiles. 453 
Accordingly, there is evidence of high dependence on negative oil movements in bearish periods 454 
for each of the clean energy indices whereas there is a high dependency on positive oil 455 
movements during bullish periods. Economically speaking, a potential explanation for these 456 
results is that the high negative oil returns in bearish market conditions seem to decrease the 457 
investment in crude oil and thus investors choose better assets to hedge market risk. As negative 458 
market movement has a high impact on clean energy sources, investors put more money into 459 
clean energy sources to hedge their portfolios. However, during bullish market conditio ns, rising 460 
oil prices move symmetrically with clean energy stocks implying the importance of alternative 461 
energy as a superior source of energy.  462 
 463 
26 
 
The results of this empirical research have significant implications to investors, financial 464 
managers, and portfolio managers who intend to estimate portfolio risk during the episodes of oil 465 
price shocks. Our results are also important for detecting hedging and arbitrage opportunities in 466 
alternative energy firms. To this end, investors holding assets in crude oil and clean energy 467 
markets could use those findings when constructing minimum risk portfolios. Policymakers 468 
could also make use of our outcomes while formulating effective strategies to moderate the 469 
impact of oil price shocks on clean energy stocks. One such  policy could be encouraging 470 
industries to improve efficiency in the usage of crude oil and to employ alternative sources to 471 
evade oscillations in clean energy stock prices. In sum, active measures should be taken into 472 
account for avoiding the contagion risk originating from the unstable crude oil market. 473

5. Conclusions and implications for sustainability  475 
Unlike previous researchers who mostly apply mean -based models, we examine the dependence 476 
structure between WTI prices and renewable energy equity indexe s using quantile regression to 477 
offer a more comprehensive dependence structure under diverse market conditions. Our 478 
empirical analyses provide solid evidence for the decreasing dependence of clean energy stocks 479 
on oil price movements. Additionally, the lag ged effect of WTI prices on clean energy equity 480 
returns is generally significant, which indicates that clean energy stock returns react differently 481 
to new information on oil returns under different market conditions. Further analyses involving 482 
the presence of asymmetry in the relationship between crude oil returns on the returns of clean 483 
energy stock indices in various market conditions show strong effects of negative oil returns 484 
during bearish periods and an insignificant effect during bullish periods.   485 
Understanding the connectedness between renewable energy stocks and traditional energy prices 486 
is of paramount importance to ethical investors, as this information is essential for gaining 487 
superior risk -adjusted returns through proper allocation of clean ene rgy assets to a portfolio. 488 
Such knowledge further helps to identify whether and to what extent these stocks are sensitive to 489 
shocks emanating from other allied markets.  It is worth mentioning that although ethical 490 
investors aim at decarbonizing their portf olios, they still attempt to receive healthy returns from 491 
their investments. If decarbonizing portfolios does not provide incentives for switching over to 492 
renewable energy sources, investors would be reluctant to green their portfolios which will 493 
27 
 
hamper the migration towards a low -carbon economy. Our findings will be of particular interest 494 
to those participants who want to invest in eco -friendly firms. Overall, these results could be 495 
useful in outlining sustainable business strategies and designing optimal portfolios. 496 
Given earlier evidence from the academic literature that investments in clean energy stocks have 497 
positive environmental and socio -economic impacts that potentially help ensure a certain degree 498 
of sustainability, it is thus important to consider the application of modern portfolio theory with a 499 
view to gaining proper knowledge of stock market strategies. Accordingly, our current empirical 500 
analysis is of interest to institutional investors who aim at detecting the clean energy market risk  501 
via proper financial modelling. Furthermore, and g iven the growing interest in both alternative 502 
fuel sources and alternative investments, understanding the relationship between oil and clean 503 
energy stock markets is always crucial for making investment and risk management decisions.  504 
For future research, it would be interesting to examine the impact of crude oil implied volatility 505 
on the return behaviour of renewable energy stocks under diverse market conditions. Other 506 
extensions could involve  the potential  association between critical metals and the markets of 507 
clean energy technologies [34] or the application of the approach of Kazemilari et al. [2] on the 508 
oil-clean energy nexus.  509
