Abstract

This   paper   tests  whether    common      intraday    momentum       signals   built  from    open,   high,  low,   close,   and  volume
(OHLCV) data generate economically meaningful edge in Micro E-Mini Nasdaq 100 futures (MNQ) after realistic
transaction costs. Fourteen signal families were evaluated on 947 trading days of five-minute bar data spanning 2021
through    2025,     under    expanding-window          walk-forward       validation.    Each    signal    had   to   clear   five   criteria
simultaneously to pass: a T-statistic of at least 2.0 on out-of-sample (OOS) net returns, at least 30 trades per OOS
fold, a positive net return after instrument-appropriate round-trip friction, consistent direction across the three OOS
test years (2023, 2024, and 2025), and a permutation test p-value below 0.05 where applicable. None passed all five.

The failures do not reduce to a single cause. Eleven signal families fail because their maximum gross return before
friction is 0.07 to 1.50 points per trade, below the 2.0-point MNQ friction floor. Three families clear the friction gate
but fail  elsewhere: the     Opening     Range Breakout long at a 15-bar hold (net +2.82 points, T = 0.88 on net returns)
fails the T-statistic and year-stability gates; the VVG (Volatility-Volume-Gap) classifier reversal (net +13.49 points,
T  = 1.26   on 35 total OOS trades) fails the same two; and gap continuation short (gross +16.53 points, net +14.52
points, T = 1.46) clears friction and approaches the T-statistic threshold but fails year stability and the per-fold trade
count minimum. Earlier versions of this paper collapsed all fourteen failures into a single cost-barrier narrative. That
framing was inaccurate for three of the families.

Two    signals   from    a  companion       research    program     serve    as  positive    controls:   the   RTH     Confluence      Signal
(walk-forward OOS T = 3.11, mean net +11.82 points, N = 196; unconditional long benchmark over the in-sample
period 2022–2024:       T  = −0.75, mean net −2.60 points on 759 trades) and London Session Signal B (walk-forward
OOS    T  =   4.30, mean net +4.09 points, N           = 247,   p  = 0.000025).      Both clear    all five criteria with margin.       They
demonstrate that the validation protocol is not simply rejecting everything — it can detect genuine edge when the
underlying architecture differs from single-bar OHLCV pattern prediction.

Several implementation details relevant to replication are documented explicitly in this version: the 20-bar ATR for
the RTH Confluence Signal           is computed      on  1-minute    bars  and   merged    to  the  5-minute    pipeline at the bar close
timestamp; the RTH GMM (Gaussian Mixture Model) was fit once on 2022 data only and applied forward without
per-fold refitting; the    London     GMM was refit at each walk-forward fold; and the continuous contract uses simple
quarterly file concatenation with no roll adjustment. Each of these points generated direct replication questions from
external researchers who attempted to reproduce the signals.

1. Introduction

This project began as a trading strategy search, not a falsification study. Several months of testing individual signals
—   each    failing   under   out-of-sample       evaluation    and    transaction    costs   —    made    the  pattern   clear   enough     that
documenting      the   failures  systematically seemed more            productive    than   continuing    to search    for exceptions.      That
reframing    is the  actual   origin   of  this paper,   and it   matters   because    a paper that started       as a  search has different
epistemics than one designed ab initio as a null-result study.

MNQ was chosen for practical reasons: widely accessible data, tight bid-ask spreads, and sufficient liquidity to make
the  friction assumptions       here  conservative rather than optimistic. The signals tested are the ones that appear most
frequently    in retail  futures   trading   discussions — opening range breakouts, gap fills, volume spikes, Asia session
momentum, news-driven drift. Almost none survive a properly constructed test, and the reasons vary by signal type
in ways that the aggregate summary in the abstract cannot convey.

The   Efficient    Market    Hypothesis,      formalized     by   Fama     (1970),   predicts    this  general    outcome.     In  competitive
markets,   any publicly observable pattern that reliably generates excess returns gets arbitraged away until expected
gross  return approximately equals          the cost of exploitation. On five-minute OHLCV bars in a contract as liquid as
MNQ, that cost sits around one to two points per trade. The results here are consistent with that equilibrium — not
as a logical consequence of EMH, but as an empirical observation that EMH renders unsurprising. The distinction
matters:   Fama    (1970)    is cited  as  interpretive context,      not as a deductive premise from which the specific friction
level is derived. Gao et al. (2018) document intraday momentum in ETF markets, and Heston et al. (2010) examine
intraday return patterns in equities; both establish that predictability, when it exists, tends to operate at short horizons
with specific structural conditions that differ from simple OHLCV patterns. That is the literature this paper speaks
to.

The paper is     organized as follows.        Section   2  describes the data       and execution framework.          Section    3 defines the
validation criteria and the null hypothesis behind the T-statistics used throughout. Section 4 documents each signal
family. Section 5 presents the positive controls. Section 6 interprets the gross edge ceiling. Sections 7 and 8 cover
limitations and conclusions. Appendix A provides replication specifications for both positive controls.


2. Data and Execution Framework

2.1 Data
The primary      dataset is 72,604 five-minute OHLCV bars for MNQ continuous front-month futures, regular trading
hours   only   (RTH,    09:30–16:00 ET),        December      2021    through    August    2025. After removing partial          sessions    and
zero-volume bars, 947 complete trading days remain. Source is NinjaTrader, aggregated from one-minute raw bars.
The continuous contract is built by simple concatenation of quarterly files with duplicate timestamp removal at roll
dates. There is no price back-adjustment and no explicit holiday calendar — holidays appear as missing bars after
resampling     and   are   excluded     implicitly   through    dropna.    Lopez    de   Prado    (2018)   discusses     the  implications     of
roll-date price discontinuities for backtesting; the present study’s signals rely primarily on relative price movements
and volume z-scores rather than absolute price levels, which partially mitigates but does not eliminate this concern.

MGC (Micro Gold Futures) five-minute bars spanning 1,091 days were used for the cross-instrument test in Section
4.8. Asia session tests used 20:00–02:00 ET bars from the same MNQ contract.

2.2 Execution Assumptions
Signal at bar close. Entry at next           bar open. This convention eliminates the fill-price error that afflicts most retail
backtests, where the strategy executes at a price that was only visible in hindsight.

Round-trip friction costs are instrument-specific. For MNQ: 2.0 points ($4.00 per micro contract), covering bid-ask
spread, NinjaTrader exchange fees, and conservative slippage. For MGC: 0.50 points round-trip — 5 ticks at $1.00
per tick per contract — reflecting that instrument’s different contract specification. Applying MNQ’s nominal point
cost to MGC would impose a materially heavier friction burden (MGC’s 2.0-point equivalent is $20.00 per contract
versus MNQ’s $4.00) and was the source of a methodological error in earlier drafts. All net figures in this paper are
after instrument-appropriate friction.

3. Validation Standards

All T-statistics in this paper are one-sample, two-sided t-tests under a null hypothesis of zero mean net return:






                                                               Mesfin (2026)  |  3
                                                               T = μ ₑ     / (SD / √N)

where μ ₑ  is the mean net return after friction, SD is the standard deviation of net returns across OOS trades, and
N  is the   number of trades in the fold. A T-statistic of 2.0 corresponds approximately to p < 0.05 two-sided at the
sample sizes used here. All T-statistics are computed on net returns. This matters because an earlier version of this
paper reported T-statistics on gross returns for the volume signals while showing net returns in the mean column —
producing a sign inconsistency that external reviewers correctly identified. That error is corrected throughout.

A signal passes only if it clears all five of the following simultaneously:

                                                                                             after is not deployable.

                                     2024, 2025                                      negative ones is not a structural edge.

The   sequential     gating structure matters.         Signals eliminated       at  the  gross-edge or       T-statistic   gates never reach the
permutation      test.  This   is  by  design,    not   oversight:     a  permutation      test  on   a  signal   with T =     0.3 would add        no
information. The permutation criterion is included in the protocol because it applies to signals that survive all prior
gates — both positive controls clear it with p < 0.001.


4. Signal Families Tested

Fourteen signal families were tested across eight subsections. The mapping: ORB (Section 4.1, three variants), Asia
expansion     (4.2),   Asia   liquidity    grab   (4.3),   gap   fill and    gap   continuation      (4.4,  two   families),    volume     spike   and
volume dry-up (4.5, two families), VVG classifier reversal, continuation, and close fade (4.6, three families counted
as one family group), event day trend (4.7), and MGC OU mean reversion (4.8). Subsection length varies with the
complexity of what each result requires to interpret.

The mechanism         is timing.   Expansion bursts are real — the directional move exists — but it is entirely consumed

next bar open is +32.24 points in the expansion direction, while the mean return from bar close to next bar open is

4.3 Asia Session Liquidity Grab Reversal
6,442 events were identified where price temporarily pierced a session extreme before closing back inside the range.
Long grabs: 3,419. Short grabs: 3,023.

Fading the grab direction: mean net -2.20 points, T = -14.12 on net returns. Trading with the grab: mean net -1.80
points, T    = -13.24. Both      directions fail. The gross directional content in either direction is 0.20 to 0.80 points —
smaller than friction in both cases. This is the clearest pure friction ceiling result in the study.

4.7 Event Day Trend
Major    economic      releases    —    FOMC      (Federal    Open     Market    Committee),       CPI   (Consumer       Price   Index),    NFP
(Nonfarm     Payrolls),   PCE    (Personal    Consumption Expenditures)            — hypothetically generate directional order flow
from   forced institutional repositioning. The ForexFactory                calendar used here contains 993 qualifying high-impact
USD events from 2022 to 2025. This count reflects all high-impact events in the calendar, not only the four named
types. NFP, CPI, and PCE are all released at 08:30 ET and fall outside RTH hours entirely. FOMC decisions (14:00
ET)   and   a  range    of  other   high-impact      releases    (GDP,    retail  sales,   ISM,   etc.)  constitute    the  RTH-compatible
subsample.

From bar +6 onward, T-statistics on net returns range from 0.14 to 0.69 across all horizons. The 2025 partial year
shows 12 trades at mean net -9.56 points. No persistent post-news drift materializes once the initial spike bars are
excluded.

5. Positive Control Signals

Both   signals   were developed as         part of a    companion research          program and       are included here strictly as positive
controls. Full replication specifications are in Appendix A, which exists because external researchers attempting to
reproduce these      signals    encountered      ambiguity on several implementation points                 not stated     in earlier manuscript
versions.

5.1 RTH Confluence Signal




                                                                Mesfin (2026)  |  9
The signal fires when three conditions are simultaneously true on a completed five-minute bar: the GMM (Gaussian
Mixture Model) regime label equals Regime 1 (Active Flow), the rolling 200-bar Markov transition probability from
Regime    1  to Regime 2 exceeds 0.15, and the rolling 50-bar volume z-score exceeds 0.5. The Markov probability
here is a first-order estimated transition rate derived from a rolling count matrix applied to prior bars only, with no
lookahead.     The   GMM      uses four    features and     was fit once on 2022 data, then applied              forward without per-fold
refitting  —   a design choice that contaminates the W1                OOS    fold (2022 H2), since the model was trained on data
overlapping that test period. Entry is on a 25-point ATR-scaled pullback. The ATR is a 20-bar average true range
computed on 1-minute bars, then merged to the 5-minute pipeline at the bar close timestamp. This is not the same as
computing ATR on 5-minute bars directly — the 1-minute computation has higher temporal resolution and was the
source of replication errors in every external attempt before the detail was disclosed.

An unconditional long benchmark — enter long at 09:30 every trading day, exit at bar 13, 2022–2024 — produces
mean net -2.60 points, T = -0.75 on 759 trades. The RTH Confluence signal’s OOS excess return is not a product of
passive directional drift in MNQ during the early session.

6.2 The Competitive Interpretation
MNQ is among the most liquid futures contracts in the world. Any OHLCV pattern visible to all market participants
that reliably predicts the next bar gets traded away. Participants with lower costs and faster execution take the other
side  until  gross edge      approximately      equals the friction       cost of the marginal participant. On five-minute bars, that
equilibrium appears        to sit  near one     to two points gross — an empirical observation consistent with Fama (1970),
though not logically derived from it.

The two positive controls exist outside this ceiling because they use GMM regime classification and hold positions
for 60 to 75 minutes. Regime transitions derived from multi-feature Gaussian Mixture Models are less transparently
observable     than raw     OHLCV patterns,         and   the longer hold period accumulates enough net return to clear friction
before    the   edge    reverts.   The    ceiling    applies    specifically     to  single-bar     directional    predictions     from    publicly
observable OHLCV features, not to all intraday strategies.

7. Limitations and Extensions

7.1 Limitations
Four limitations are material to how the positive-control results should be interpreted.

First, the  RTH     Confluence Signal       specification     was selected after      evaluating 53 or more parameter combinations
across seven parameters. The walk-forward OOS test was run once on the locked specification, which is the correct
procedure, but the prior search creates multiple-testing exposure that cannot be corrected within the existing dataset.
Bailey   &   Lopez    de   Prado    (2014)   and   Bailey    et  al. (2017)    both   provide    frameworks      for   quantifying    this risk;
applying either to the present search           scope would reduce the effective statistical confidence in the OOS T = 3.11
result.

Second, the     RTH GMM was fit on full 2022 data, and the W1 OOS fold tests on 2022 H2. That fold’s results are
mildly   contaminated.      The combined       OOS     T =   3.11   aggregates a contaminated fold with two clean ones; the true
OOS T across only the clean folds is higher but based on fewer trades.

Third, fold-by-fold unconditional long benchmarks for the positive controls’ OOS periods were not computed. The
2022–2024 full-period benchmark (T = -0.75 for RTH; T = -0.22 for London) confirms the signals’ excess return is
not passive    drift  over   the  in-sample period, but the same comparison has not been made for the OOS folds. This
remains genuinely open.

Fourth, the two-point MNQ friction assumption is fixed in nominal terms. MNQ traded between roughly 11,000 and
24,000 during the sample period, so 2.0 points represents approximately 1.8 to 0.8 basis points depending on price
level. Whether the gross edge ceiling shifts proportionally across price levels is untested.



                                                             Mesfin (2026)  |  14
7.2 Extensions
Gap continuation short is the most specific candidate for future work. With MNQ data back to 2019, approximately
three additional years of data would roughly triple the per-fold OOS trade count and enable a meaningful validation.
The 2023 and 2025 partial results both strongly positive, the mechanism coherent — this is worth testing properly
when more data exists.

Tick data is the highest-priority data extension for the broader signal set. The Asia session expansion bar finding and
the London Signal B delay sensitivity both have mechanistic explanations that require sub-bar resolution to test or
falsify. Databento offers MNQ tick data at approximately $42 per quarter.


8. Conclusion

Fourteen signal families. 947 trading days. Consistent methodology applied across all of them. Eleven fail because
gross edge cannot clear friction. Three clear friction and fail on T-statistic, year stability, or per-fold sample size.

Gap continuation short came closest. Gross +16.53 points, net +14.52, T = 1.46. It fails two criteria simultaneously,
but both failures are of the kind that more data could address. That is a different category from the eleven families
whose gross edge is structurally too small to matter.

The   two   positive    controls   pass   because    they   use   regime detection       and longer     hold periods     —    architecture    that
escapes the gross edge ceiling that eliminates OHLCV pattern signals at five-minute resolution. The ceiling is an
empirical observation consistent with competitive market dynamics, not a theoretical proof of impossibility. But at
this resolution, on this instrument, with this data type, the barrier is real enough that every signal family tested here
ran into it.
